# App corpus: robust rendering (traps, memory, time)

October 15, 2026. Rendering on the corpus's hostile files (`reference/corpus-20261015/`, baseline `summary-426a2c6.json`) never traps and stays within memory and time budgets. Fixed in the patched hayro crates (`vendor/hayro-0.8.0/`, `vendor/hayro-interpret-0.8.0/`, each with `PATCH.md`), `src/view/` and the app's view check (`src/app/view_cost.rs`). Tests: `tests/view_robust.rs`, unit tests in `src/app/view_cost.rs`.

## Why patched crates

- The traps panic inside hayro and vello on sizes hayro derives while painting (a tint transform's output count, a pattern step scaled by the CTM). A tint transform's output count can depend on its input (`{dup 0.5 gt {0} {0 0 0 0} ifelse}`), and step pixmaps depend on the CTM at the paint, so no rewrite of the PDF in the view's overlay prevents them. Refusing the page would drop pages MuPDF draws.
- hayro and hayro-interpret 0.8.0 are vendored with the smallest patches found (every change marked `Tandem patch`), wired with `[patch.crates-io]` in `Cargo.toml` and `bridge/Cargo.toml` (both lockfiles drop the registry source of the two crates). `DEPENDENCIES.md` and `NOTICE` record them. No new dependency; no unsafe code.

## Root causes (corpus files by source, path and SHA-256 prefix)

- **Separation/DeviceN traps** (veraPDF `PDF_A-4/.../6-2-4-4-t01-pass-h.pdf` 18e82c5dad66 and six siblings): a tint transform leaving more (or fewer) values than its alternate space has components was passed on; `DeviceRgb::convert` panicked copying 1,024 bytes into 768. Values are now cut or padded with 0 to the alternate's count, and a PostScript function's outputs are the top n of its stack (n from /Range), as MuPDF 1.28.1 and pdf.js take them.
- **Tiling trap** (pdf.js `tiling-pattern-large-steps.pdf` 10df93162f8b): a 90,000-unit step became a 65,535-pixel step pixmap edge (`u16` cast), 17 GB for both axes; vello panicked snapping tiles at the 16-bit edge (`vello_common` `util.rs:237`). Step pixmaps are bounded (budgets below); this file's single visible cell is drawn alone.
- **Memory, tiling** (pdf.js `tiling-pattern-box.pdf` b67ae1bd9c80, past 1.5 GB): 63 patterns each holding one 596 × 842 cell every 2,876 units (a writer painting images through one-cell patterns); each fill kept a 2,876² step pixmap until the page was composed. Where the steps leave space between cells and the area drawn meets one cell only, that cell is drawn alone as a padded image on the lattice.
- **Memory and time, images** (pdf.js `issue16263.pdf` 99548e25da25, 18–22 s): a 2 × 2 image whose one-bit soft mask has 34,862 × 4,332 samples, drawn many times at about 100 pixels: every draw expanded the mask to a byte per sample and resampled it. Images and masks drawn more than 8× smaller than their samples are reduced by whole-number box factors when decoded (a one-bit mask counted from its bits), and a decoded image is kept for the rest of the render.
- **Time, text** (govdocs1 `000748.pdf` 3683a7c0e58d: page 0 29 s, `text-all` past 25 s, 1.1 GB natively): every marked-content sequence found its structure element by walking the page's /StructParents array from its start (`Array::iter().nth(mcid)`), resolving every element before it: quadratic in MCIDs. The render request also extracts the page's text, so the render paid it too. The array is now read once per page and only the named element resolved (`stext::Parents`). Natively 27.2 s → 0.19 s for the whole file's text.
- **Memory, view open** (app-view.md's open item): hayro parses every page's /Resources and seven subdictionaries into maps when the view opens; pages sharing a large resources object cost pages × entries (1,000 × 4,000 fonts: 365 MB). `view_cost::over_budget` sums those entries before hayro opens the view and refuses it past `MAX_VIEW_ENTRIES` (`ViewError::TooComplex`, the app's render error).
- **Memory, content decoding** (pdf.js `test/pdfs/bomb_giant.pdf`, not on the baseline's failing list only because its 1,536 MB ceiling was above its 1,447 MB peak; natively past 1.5 GB in 0.4 s on the base commit too): the page's /Contents holds three streams encoded with /JBIG2Decode whose segments declare 100,000 × 100,000-pixel pages; hayro decodes content whole before reading it. Content through an image filter (JBIG2, JPX, DCT, CCITT fax) yields samples, not operators, and MuPDF draws nothing from it. The app's view leaves such page streams out of /Contents in its private update (`app::view_contents`); the content walk refuses a page, form, pattern or glyph procedure whose content is so encoded before anything decodes it (`view::walk`, `TooComplex`). The file now renders in 16 MB natively, matching MuPDF within 1,097 edge pixels.
- **render-dims** (pyHanko `.../page-tree-direct-kid.pdf` 28d12784c087): a page written directly into its parent's /Kids reads /Parent from inside the object that holds it; hayro's reader took the reference for a cycle, so the page lost its inherited MediaBox and drew as Letter. `view::inherited` resolves /Parent through the xref itself.
- **render-dims** (qpdf `qpdf/qtest/qpdf/issue-99.pdf` 7613e0aa0732, not changed): a fuzzed file whose last definition of the page object is a damaged dictionary. MuPDF drops the whole dictionary (the page has no keys, Letter); the engine's parser keeps the keys around the damage, including `/MediaBox [0 0 595 842]`, and Rust's page size, render and save follow it. Parser leniency belongs to the open piece; both readings are defensible and Rust's keeps more of the file.
- **Slow renders** (`slowestRustRenders`): the two above over 10× MuPDF are fixed (000748 pages 0 and 1, issue16263); `tiling-pattern-box` and IRS `fw4.pdf` page 4 measured below 10× after (see Measurements).

## Budgets

| Budget | Value | Where | Why | Past it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `MAX_VIEW_ENTRIES` | 2^22 entries | `app::view_cost` | hayro's per-page resource maps, about 90 bytes an entry (380 MB) | the view is refused: `TooComplex`, the render error |
| `MAX_RENDER_EDGE` | 65,280 px (the last whole 256-pixel wide tile) | `view::render` | vello rounds tiles up in 16-bit coordinates and panicked drawing at the end of a 65,535-pixel edge | `BadScale`, as for `MAX_RENDER_PIXELS` |
| `MAX_RENDER_PIXMAP_PIXELS` | 2^27 px (512 MiB RGBA) per render | hayro `GlobalState` | vello keeps every image and pattern pixmap until the page is composed | each further pixmap reduced to at most 4,096 px |
| `MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` | 2^25 px (128 MiB RGBA), 32,767 a side | hayro `image.rs` | one image pixmap; widths past 65,535 were cut to 16 bits | drawn reduced |
| `MAX_TILE_PIXELS`, `MAX_TILE_EDGE` | 2^24 px (64 MiB), 16,384 a side | hayro `paint.rs` | one tiling step pixmap; edges inside vello's coordinates | cell drawn at a reduced scale |
| `MAX_UNPACKED_SAMPLES` | 2^28 samples | hayro-interpret decode | 1–7 and 9–15-bit images are unpacked into their declared size, missing data padded | image not drawn |
| `MAX_MESH_TRIANGLES`, `MAX_MESH_PATCHES` | 2^18 triangles (about 60 MB), 2^14 patches | hayro-interpret `shading.rs` | a mesh is read whole, a few bits per triangle or patch | rest of the mesh not drawn |
| `MAX_SAMPLE_WORK` | 2^26 pixel tests per mesh paint (about 0.5 s natively) | hayro-interpret `encode.rs` | each triangle is sampled over its bounding box; 2^18 page-sized triangles took 40 s | rest of the mesh not drawn |
| patch grid | a point every 4 device pixels, 2 to 20 a side | hayro-interpret `encode.rs` | hayro cut every patch into 722 triangles whatever its size | (none: small patches get fewer triangles) |
| content through an image filter | none decoded | `app::view_contents`, `view::walk` | JBIG2 page content asked for 1.5 GB | page streams left out of the app's view; other such content refused (`TooComplex`) |
| `REDUCED_CACHE_BYTES` | 32 MiB per render | hayro-interpret decode | decoded images kept so repeated draws decode once | further images decoded per draw (as before) |
| box reduction | more than 8× | hayro, hayro-interpret | resampling work follows the output | the resampler does the last 4–8× |

Tests set `RenderSettings::max_pixmap_pixels` small through `ViewDocument::render_with_pixmap_budget` (doc-hidden) and `over_budget`'s limit argument; the other constants are proved by inputs that stay cheap for the unpatched renderer (below).

## Differences from MuPDF

- Tint transforms: values past the alternate's components are dropped, missing ones are 0; a PostScript function yields the top n stack values (n from /Range), 0 for missing ones. Where the function leaves too few values MuPDF 1.28.1 draws stale values (the colour of an earlier, unrelated document in the same process, measured), so it is no oracle there.
- A tiling cell is clipped to its /BBox when drawn alone; MuPDF draws pdf.js `tiling-pattern-large-steps.pdf`'s cell 50 units past its BBox (the file's own comment asks for 50 units of padding on every side; Rust has them). 19,850 pixels differ at scale 1.
- Images and masks reduced more than 8× go through a box filter first: thin lines in them come out lighter than MuPDF's (issue16263's one-sample arrows; the file stays off the failing list).
- Past the render's pixmap budget images and pattern cells are drawn reduced (blurred); MuPDF draws each at full resolution. Past the mesh budgets the rest of a mesh is not drawn; MuPDF draws all of it. Past `MAX_UNPACKED_SAMPLES` an image is not drawn; MuPDF tries to allocate it. A document past `MAX_VIEW_ENTRIES` cannot be rendered.
- A lattice mesh's /VerticesPerRow below 2, or missing, reads as 2 (MuPDF warns and does the same; hayro drew nothing or looped).
- A Coons or tensor-product patch is cut into triangles on a grid of a point every 4 device pixels (at most hayro's 20 × 20), so its colours are interpolated linearly over up to 4 pixels; MuPDF subdivides by its own measure. The oracle's Coons case matches within 2 levels at scales 1 and 3.
- qpdf `issue-99.pdf`: Rust's page is 595 × 842 where MuPDF's is Letter (above).
- Page content streams through an image filter are left out of the app's view; MuPDF decodes them and finds no operators (same drawing; a crafted JBIG2 stream that decodes to operators would draw in MuPDF only). A form, pattern, glyph procedure or appearance so encoded makes the page a render error; MuPDF draws the rest of the page.

## Sweep and generated cases

- Read along the paths these files hit for `copy_from_slice`, `unwrap` and indexing on data-derived lengths: colour conversions (`ToRgb` for every space: only `DeviceRgb` copied without a length check; `Indexed` lookups are bounded by `hival` and the table it built; ICC transforms return errors), function evaluation (type 0 lookups use `get`; type 4 outputs above), mesh readers (lattice loop without end at zero vertices per row; patch flags index fixed arrays), tiling and image pixmap sizes (`u16` casts above), the resampler (`unwrap`s above) and vello's 16-bit tile snapping (render edge above). The decoders size buffers from declared image sizes only in the unpack paths above; DCT data is patched down to the dictionary's size by hayro-syntax, JBIG2 bitmaps are capped at 65,535 a side.
- A scratch harness (not in the repository) rendered generated pages at scales 0.5 and 2 with text extraction, under the memory cap: colour spaces of every family nested three deep with functions of every type, shading types 1–7 with random parameters and mesh data, tiling patterns with steps from 0 and 0.001 to ±1e30, degenerate, inverted and huge cells, singular and 1e30 matrices, images of 0 to 100,000 samples a side at 0–32 bits with masks, /Matte, colour-key arrays and /Decode arrays of every length (four generators, 2,000 cases each for five seeds); and valid meshes of types 4–7 with up to 200 KB of data, 1 to 32 bits a coordinate, zero to 70,000 vertices per row and coordinates up to 10^6 (three seeds of 1,000). Along the way it found the lattice loop (zero vertices per row: past 1 GB in a second), the declared-size unpacking (100,000 × 100,000 samples), a DeviceN image evaluating its tint transform per padded pixel (3 s for a 25-byte stream) and mesh sampling without a work bound (up to 40 s a case); all are fixed above. With every fix in place: 43,000 cases, no panic; no case of the four generators past 2 s; mesh cases at most 2.4 s for four mesh paints, two renders and the text. One case's memory rose by at most about 340 MB (several 64 MiB step pixmaps of sparse patterns with several cells visible, inside the tile and render budgets) and about 180 MB for the largest meshes (2^18 triangles kept by the shading caches).
- `tests/view_robust.rs` keeps bounded versions (images 2 × 2, meshes under 200 bytes, at least one vertex per row, steps up to 100 and cells whose unpatched step pixmap stays near 1,000 pixels a side), so the suite stays cheap even if a patch is lost. Every test was also run against the unpatched crates under the memory cap: each finished within a second at a polled peak under 4 MB; the trap, budget, lattice, negative-step and mesh-budget tests fail there, the others (behaviour kept by the patches, or fixes in the engine's own code) pass.

## Gaps and decisions

- **Decompression bombs.** hayro-syntax decodes Flate, LZW and the other filters without an output limit, for content streams, images, fonts and functions alike; a stream of a few kilobytes can decode to gigabytes, and nested filters multiply that (JBIG2 image streams decode at the size their segments declare, up to 65,535² bits a bitmap). Only content through image filters is guarded here. On wasm32 an allocation failure aborts the engine. Bounding it needs a third patched crate (an output budget in `hayro-syntax`'s filters) or decoding through the engine's own bounded filters. Decision needed.
- **Soft masks** are rendered at the page's size and kept per distinct mask until the page is composed (1 byte a pixel each); a page with many distinct soft masks at a large render size costs masks × pixels. Not bounded here.
- Each paint of a mesh shading samples it afresh (hayro keeps no sampled mesh), so a page painting one large mesh many times costs up to `MAX_SAMPLE_WORK` a paint.
- Sampled (type 0) functions with many inputs evaluate 2^inputs table corners; with a large enough table a DeviceN image in such a space is slow. Not reached by the corpus.
- The pixmap budget degrades rather than fails, so a render past it is not reported to the app; `RenderCache` could expose the count if the app wants to say so.
