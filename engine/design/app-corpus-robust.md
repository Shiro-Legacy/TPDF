# App corpus: robust rendering (traps, memory, time)

October 15, 2026. Rendering on the corpus's hostile files (`reference/corpus-20261015/`, baseline `summary-426a2c6.json`) never traps and stays within memory and time budgets. Fixed in the patched hayro crates (`vendor/hayro-0.8.0/`, `vendor/hayro-interpret-0.8.0/`, each with `PATCH.md`), `src/view/` and the app's view check (`src/app/view_cost.rs`). Tests: `tests/view_robust.rs`, unit tests in `src/app/view_cost.rs`, and on the WASM build `tests/rust-engine-robust.test.mjs` (app root, `npm run test:rust`): 32-bit size arithmetic is invisible to native tests. Review fixes (second commit): 32-bit overflows, decoders sized from declared sizes, JPX double reduction, budgets that refuse instead of dropping images, a hard pixmap ceiling with shared pixmaps, a scene budget, the view's byte budget, lazy structure parents.

## Why patched crates

- The traps panic inside hayro and vello on sizes hayro derives while painting (a tint transform's output count, a pattern step scaled by the CTM). A tint transform's output count can depend on its input (`{dup 0.5 gt {0} {0 0 0 0} ifelse}`), and step pixmaps depend on the CTM at the paint, so no rewrite of the PDF in the view's overlay prevents them. Refusing the page would drop pages MuPDF draws.
- hayro and hayro-interpret 0.8.0 are vendored with the smallest patches found (every change marked `Tandem patch`), wired with `[patch.crates-io]` in `Cargo.toml` and `bridge/Cargo.toml` (both lockfiles drop the registry source of the two crates). `DEPENDENCIES.md` and `NOTICE` record them. No new dependency; no unsafe code.

## Root causes (corpus files by source, path and SHA-256 prefix)

- **Separation/DeviceN traps** (veraPDF `PDF_A-4/.../6-2-4-4-t01-pass-h.pdf` 18e82c5dad66 and six siblings): a tint transform leaving more (or fewer) values than its alternate space has components was passed on; `DeviceRgb::convert` panicked copying 1,024 bytes into 768. Values are now cut or padded with 0 to the alternate's count, and a PostScript function's outputs are the top n of its stack (n from /Range), as MuPDF 1.28.1 and pdf.js take them.
- **Tiling trap** (pdf.js `tiling-pattern-large-steps.pdf` 10df93162f8b): a 90,000-unit step became a 65,535-pixel step pixmap edge (`u16` cast), 17 GB for both axes; vello panicked snapping tiles at the 16-bit edge (`vello_common` `util.rs:237`). Step pixmaps are bounded (budgets below); this file's single visible cell is drawn alone.
- **Memory, tiling** (pdf.js `tiling-pattern-box.pdf` b67ae1bd9c80, past 1.5 GB): 63 patterns each holding one 596 × 842 cell every 2,876 units (a writer painting images through one-cell patterns); each fill kept a 2,876² step pixmap until the page was composed. Where the steps leave space between cells and the area drawn meets one cell only, that cell is drawn alone as a padded image on the lattice.
- **Memory and time, images** (pdf.js `issue16263.pdf` 99548e25da25, 18–22 s): a 2 × 2 image whose one-bit soft mask has 34,862 × 4,332 samples, drawn many times at about 100 pixels: every draw expanded the mask to a byte per sample and resampled it. Images and masks drawn more than 8× smaller than their samples are reduced by whole-number box factors when decoded (a one-bit mask counted from its bits), and a decoded image is kept for the rest of the render. The factors follow the decoded image's own size: a JPX decoder that already skipped resolution levels (or a JPEG smaller than its dictionary) is not reduced again (the first commit reduced it twice; pdf.js `issue19326.pdf` fd41067a652c at scale 0.25 agreed with MuPDF on 48.6% of pixels, base 66.5%, now 67.6%).
- **WASM traps from 32-bit sizes** (review; synthetic): `fix_image_length` computed `row × height` in `usize`, which wraps on wasm32, cutting a 16 × 2^30 image's data to nothing while its height stayed; the box filters then divided by or indexed past the wrapped sizes. Sizes are now u64 or checked, data that does not hold its size is left unreduced, and every image hayro-interpret hands the renderer holds width × height × components bytes within the image budget.
- **Decoders sized from declared sizes** (review; synthetic): zune-jpeg's output size overflowed `usize` on wasm32 for a 65,535² frame and unwrapped `None`; hayro-syntax reserves `Columns × Rows` bytes for CCITT and the JBIG2 page's width × height bytes before decoding (a 749-byte CCITT image declaring 16,000² took 260 MB natively, 65,535² trapped). `x_object/decode/budget.rs` reads those sizes first (the JPEG frame, patched down to the dictionary's as hayro-syntax does, with 16-bit coefficients when progressive; CCITT parameters; JBIG2 page information, stripes, regions and referred-to segment counts; JPX image size and decomposition levels) and refuses an image past its budget before any decoder runs; a JPX image skips more resolution levels to fit instead.
- **Time, text** (govdocs1 `000748.pdf` 3683a7c0e58d: page 0 29 s, `text-all` past 25 s, 1.1 GB natively): every marked-content sequence found its structure element by walking the page's /StructParents array from its start (`Array::iter().nth(mcid)`), resolving every element before it: quadratic in MCIDs. The render request also extracts the page's text, so the render paid it too. The array is now read once per page, lazily as far as the largest MCID looked up (the first commit collected it whole on every page with /StructParents, even without marked content: 990,000 entries cost 131 MB a text extraction), and only the named element resolved (`stext::Parents`). Natively 27.2 s → 0.19 s for the whole file's text.
- **Memory and time, view open** (app-view.md's open item; review): hayro parses every page's /Resources and seven subdictionaries anew when the view opens; pages sharing a large resources object cost pages × entries in memory (1,000 × 4,000 fonts: 365 MB) and pages × bytes in time (1,000 pages sharing 2 MB of direct arrays: 33 s, few entries). `view_cost::over_budget` sums both before hayro opens the view, bytes as the view's rewrite serializes them (subdictionaries written as objects of their own counted too), and refuses it past `VIEW_LIMITS` (`ViewError::TooComplex`, the app's render error).
- **Memory, repeated paints** (review; synthetic): every draw of an image made its own pixmap, kept until the page was composed (a 64 × 64 image drawn 20,000 times: 426 MB natively, 532 MB on WASM); every paint of a function-based shading sampled a texture outside the pixmap budget (100 `sh` of a 500 × 500 page: 2.4 s, +106 MB); and vello keeps every fill, stroke and clip as strips and commands per tile row covered (20,000 page-sized fills of a 600 × 600 page: 540 MB from about 4 KB of PDF). Draws of one image at one size now share one pixmap, shadings defined by their dictionary share one texture per area, textures and soft masks count against the pixmap budget, the budget is a hard ceiling (past it the render fails), and a scene budget bounds the estimated bytes of fills, strokes and clips. A render past either is `TooComplex`, never a partial page.
- **Memory, content decoding** (pdf.js `test/pdfs/bomb_giant.pdf`, not on the baseline's failing list only because its 1,536 MB ceiling was above its 1,447 MB peak; natively past 1.5 GB in 0.4 s on the base commit too): the page's /Contents holds three streams encoded with /JBIG2Decode whose segments declare 100,000 × 100,000-pixel pages; hayro decodes content whole before reading it. Content through an image filter (JBIG2, JPX, DCT, CCITT fax) yields samples, not operators, and MuPDF draws nothing from it. The app's view leaves such page streams out of /Contents in its private update (`app::view_contents`); the content walk refuses a page, form, pattern or glyph procedure whose content is so encoded before anything decodes it (`view::walk`, `TooComplex`). The file now renders in 16 MB natively, matching MuPDF within 1,097 edge pixels.
- **render-dims** (pyHanko `.../page-tree-direct-kid.pdf` 28d12784c087): a page written directly into its parent's /Kids reads /Parent from inside the object that holds it; hayro's reader took the reference for a cycle, so the page lost its inherited MediaBox and drew as Letter. `view::inherited` resolves /Parent through the xref itself.
- **render-dims** (qpdf `qpdf/qtest/qpdf/issue-99.pdf` 7613e0aa0732, not changed): a fuzzed file whose last definition of the page object is a damaged dictionary. MuPDF drops the whole dictionary (the page has no keys, Letter); the engine's parser keeps the keys around the damage, including `/MediaBox [0 0 595 842]`, and Rust's page size, render and save follow it. Parser leniency belongs to the open piece; both readings are defensible and Rust's keeps more of the file.
- **Slow renders** (the baseline's 15 `slowestRustRenders`): the two over 10× MuPDF are fixed (000748 pages 0 and 1, issue16263); `tiling-pattern-box` and IRS `fw4.pdf` page 4 measured below 10× after (see Measurements). Outside that list two files stay past 10×, both unchanged by this work (same time on base and head natively): pdf.js `large_jpeg_downscale.pdf` 876d768b597e (page 0, a large JPEG drawn small: zune-jpeg decodes at full size where MuPDF's libjpeg decodes at 1/8; image work) and the `text-all` of veraPDF Isartor `.../6.1.12 Implementation Limits/isartor-6-1-12-t01-fail-a.pdf` a0d9fa2404be (text piece).

## Budgets

| Budget | Value | Where | Why | Past it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `VIEW_LIMITS.entries` | 2^22 entries | `app::view_cost` | hayro's per-page resource maps, about 90 bytes an entry (380 MB) | the view is refused: `TooComplex`, the render error |
| `VIEW_LIMITS.bytes` | 2^27 bytes (128 MiB) | `app::view_cost` | hayro scans each page's resources anew: 60 pages sharing 2 MB took 2.9 s natively | the view is refused (`TooComplex`) |
| `MAX_RENDER_EDGE` | 65,280 px (the last whole 256-pixel wide tile) | `view::render` | vello rounds tiles up in 16-bit coordinates and panicked drawing at the end of a 65,535-pixel edge | `BadScale`, as for `MAX_RENDER_PIXELS` |
| `MAX_RENDER_PIXMAP_PIXELS` | 2^27 px (512 MiB RGBA) per render | hayro `GlobalState` | vello keeps every image, pattern, shading and soft-mask pixmap until the page is composed (a mask's byte a pixel counts a quarter) | a pixmap is reduced to fit what remains, while at least 4,096 px remain; then the render fails (`TooComplex`) |
| `MAX_RENDER_SCENE_BYTES` | 2^30 estimated bytes per render | hayro `Renderer::scene_fits` | vello keeps every fill, stroke and clip, about 96 bytes an operation plus 64 a 4-pixel tile row and 32 a wide tile in it (measured within 20% on fills of 4, 64 and 600 px) | the render fails (`TooComplex`) |
| `MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS` | 2^25 px (128 MiB RGBA), 32,767 a side | hayro `image.rs` | one image pixmap; widths past 65,535 were cut to 16 bits | drawn reduced |
| `MAX_TILE_PIXELS`, `MAX_TILE_EDGE` | 2^24 px (64 MiB), 16,384 a side | hayro `paint.rs` | one tiling step pixmap; edges inside vello's coordinates | cell drawn at a reduced scale |
| `MAX_IMAGE_SAMPLES` | 2^28 samples (bytes) an image or mask | hayro-interpret `InterpreterSettings::max_image_samples` | the samples an image unpacks into (1–7 and 9–15 bits padded to the declared height while that fits, else the rows the data holds; 8 and 16 bits as the data holds), and what the DCT, CCITT, JBIG2 and JPX decoders allocate from declared sizes (JPX at 4 bytes a sample) | samples drawn smaller are decoded reduced a strip of rows at a time toward the size drawn (a one-bit mask counted from its bits; a JPX image skips more levels); otherwise, and for masks of more than one bit, the render fails (`TooComplex`) |
| `MAX_MESH_TRIANGLES`, `MAX_MESH_PATCHES` | 2^18 triangles (about 60 MB), 2^14 patches | hayro-interpret `shading.rs` | a mesh is read whole, a few bits per triangle or patch | rest of the mesh not drawn |
| `MAX_SAMPLE_WORK` | 2^26 pixel tests per mesh paint (about 0.5 s natively) | hayro-interpret `encode.rs` | each triangle is sampled over its bounding box; 2^18 page-sized triangles took 40 s | rest of the mesh not drawn |
| patch grid | a point every 4 device pixels, 2 to 20 a side | hayro-interpret `encode.rs` | hayro cut every patch into 722 triangles whatever its size | (none: small patches get fewer triangles) |
| content through an image filter | none decoded | `app::view_contents`, `view::walk` | JBIG2 page content asked for 1.5 GB | page streams left out of the app's view; other such content refused (`TooComplex`) |
| `REDUCED_CACHE_BYTES` | 32 MiB per render | hayro-interpret decode | decoded images kept so repeated draws decode once | further images decoded per draw (as before) |
| box reduction | more than 8× | hayro, hayro-interpret | resampling work follows the output | the resampler does the last 4–8× |

Tests set the render budgets small through `ViewDocument::render_with_budgets` (doc-hidden, `RenderBudgets`) and the view's through `over_budget`'s `Limits`; the other constants are proved by inputs that stay cheap for the unpatched renderer (below).

## Differences from MuPDF

- Tint transforms: values past the alternate's components are dropped, missing ones are 0; a PostScript function yields the top n stack values (n from /Range), 0 for missing ones. Where the function leaves too few values MuPDF 1.28.1 draws stale values (the colour of an earlier, unrelated document in the same process, measured), so it is no oracle there.
- A tiling cell is clipped to its /BBox when drawn alone; in pdf.js `tiling-pattern-large-steps.pdf` the cell's content extends 50 units past its /BBox on every side, and MuPDF does not clip it to the BBox. 19,850 pixels differ at scale 1.
- Images and masks reduced more than 8× go through a box filter first: thin lines in them come out lighter than MuPDF's (issue16263's one-sample arrows; the file stays off the failing list).
- The pixmap that crosses the render's pixmap budget is drawn reduced (blurred), and a render that needs more fails (`TooComplex`); MuPDF draws each pixmap at full resolution. A render past the scene budget fails; MuPDF composes as it goes and needs no such bound. Past the mesh budgets the rest of a mesh is not drawn; MuPDF draws all of it. A document past `VIEW_LIMITS` cannot be rendered.
- An image whose samples pass `MAX_IMAGE_SAMPLES` is decoded reduced toward the size drawn, so it is drawn (as MuPDF draws a 20,000 × 14,000 one-bit scan in about 100 MB); drawn at a sample a pixel or larger, or a mask of more than one bit, it makes the render fail where MuPDF draws it. DCT, CCITT and JBIG2 images whose declared sizes pass the budget make the render fail; MuPDF decodes them (for CCITT in a bit a pixel). JBIG2 or JPX data behind another filter cannot be read before decoding and is refused the same way.
- Many fills of one tiling pattern each render its cell (as hayro did), within the pixmap budget; MuPDF caches cells.
- A lattice mesh's /VerticesPerRow below 2, or missing, reads as 2 (MuPDF warns and does the same; hayro drew nothing or looped).
- A Coons or tensor-product patch is cut into triangles on a grid of a point every 4 device pixels (at most hayro's 20 × 20), so its colours are interpolated linearly over up to 4 pixels; MuPDF subdivides by its own measure. The oracle's Coons case matches within 2 levels at scales 1 and 3.
- qpdf `issue-99.pdf`: Rust's page is 595 × 842 where MuPDF's is Letter (above).
- Page content streams through an image filter are left out of the app's view; MuPDF decodes them and finds no operators (same drawing; a crafted JBIG2 stream that decodes to operators would draw in MuPDF only). A form, pattern, glyph procedure or appearance so encoded makes the page a render error; MuPDF draws the rest of the page.

## Sweep and generated cases

- Read along the paths these files hit for `copy_from_slice`, `unwrap` and indexing on data-derived lengths: colour conversions (`ToRgb` for every space: only `DeviceRgb` copied without a length check; `Indexed` lookups are bounded by `hival` and the table it built; ICC transforms return errors), function evaluation (type 0 lookups use `get`; type 4 outputs above), mesh readers (lattice loop without end at zero vertices per row; patch flags index fixed arrays), tiling and image pixmap sizes (`u16` casts above), the resampler (`unwrap`s above) and vello's 16-bit tile snapping (render edge above). The first commit's sweep missed the decoders that allocate from declared sizes: DCT (zune-jpeg's output, the frame patched down to the dictionary's size only when larger, so a dictionary of 65,535² passes), CCITT (`Columns × max(Rows, Height)` bytes reserved by hayro-syntax) and JBIG2 (the page's width × height bytes reserved by hayro-syntax; hayro-jbig2 caps its own bitmaps at 65,535 a side, and reserves room for a segment's referred-to segment numbers, up to 4 bytes each, before reading them); JPX allocates 4 bytes a sample at the resolution decoded. All are now read before decoding (`decode/budget.rs`), and every size product in the patched code is u64 or checked: on wasm32 `usize` wraps at 4 GiB, which no native test sees.
- A scratch harness (not in the repository) rendered generated pages at scales 0.5 and 2 with text extraction, under the memory cap: colour spaces of every family nested three deep with functions of every type, shading types 1–7 with random parameters and mesh data, tiling patterns with steps from 0 and 0.001 to ±1e30, degenerate, inverted and huge cells, singular and 1e30 matrices, images of 0 to 100,000 samples a side at 0–32 bits with masks, /Matte, colour-key arrays and /Decode arrays of every length (four generators, 2,000 cases each for five seeds); and valid meshes of types 4–7 with up to 200 KB of data, 1 to 32 bits a coordinate, zero to 70,000 vertices per row and coordinates up to 10^6 (three seeds of 1,000). Along the way it found the lattice loop (zero vertices per row: past 1 GB in a second), the declared-size unpacking (100,000 × 100,000 samples), a DeviceN image evaluating its tint transform per padded pixel (3 s for a 25-byte stream) and mesh sampling without a work bound (up to 40 s a case); all are fixed above. With every fix in place: 43,000 cases, no panic; no case of the four generators past 2 s; mesh cases at most 2.4 s for four mesh paints, two renders and the text. One case's memory rose by at most about 340 MB (several 64 MiB step pixmaps of sparse patterns with several cells visible, inside the tile and render budgets) and about 180 MB for the largest meshes (2^18 triangles kept by the shading caches).
- Review fixes are proved with small budgets (`RenderBudgets`) and small synthetic inputs: a 2^20-sample budget against 2,048² declared sizes, scene and pixmap budgets of thousands of pixels; a JPEG 2000 codestream (343 bytes) and a JPEG (367 bytes) made for the tests with `opj_compress` and `cjpeg`, and a hand-written JBIG2 page. Each new test failed with its fix reverted (the JPX, reduced-decode, shared-pixmap and declared-size tests natively; the 32-bit case on a WASM build with the u64 sizes reverted, which trapped), and each input stays under about 16 MB for the unpatched crates.
- `tests/view_robust.rs` keeps bounded versions (images 2 × 2, meshes under 200 bytes, at least one vertex per row, steps up to 100 and cells whose unpatched step pixmap stays near 1,000 pixels a side), so the suite stays cheap even if a patch is lost. Every test was also run against the unpatched crates under the memory cap: each finished within a second at a polled peak under 4 MB; the trap, budget, lattice, negative-step and mesh-budget tests fail there, the others (behaviour kept by the patches, or fixes in the engine's own code) pass.

## Gaps and decisions

- **Decompression bombs.** hayro-syntax decodes Flate, LZW and the other filters without an output limit, for content streams, images, fonts and functions alike; a stream of a few kilobytes can decode to gigabytes, and nested filters multiply that. The image decoders that allocate from declared sizes (DCT, CCITT, JBIG2, JPX) are now held to the image budget before they run, and content through image filters is guarded, but a Flate or LZW stream's own output is not bounded, nor is JBIG2 symbol and halftone decoding inside a declared page. On wasm32 an allocation failure aborts the engine. Bounding it needs a third patched crate (an output budget in `hayro-syntax`'s filters) or decoding through the engine's own bounded filters. Decided October 16 (`app-accept-integration.md`): hayro-syntax is patched with a decoding limit (128 MiB a filter, images up to their declared size within 512 MiB, page content together 128 MiB). JBIG2 symbol and halftone decoding inside a declared page is still unbounded. "Never traps" above holds for the corpus; the integration review's small hostile inputs that trapped are fixed there. The limit is per stream: decoded streams held together (forms along a chain, every page's kept content, a page's fonts) were bounded only in round 2 of that review (same note).
- **Patch size.** The two patches are larger than a minimal trap fix (see each `PATCH.md` for counts): each part closes a measured trap, memory or time problem. Upgrading hayro means re-applying them; upstreaming the size checks and budgets would shrink them.
- The scene budget estimates vello's bytes from each operation's device bounding box; a page whose operations really need more than about 1 GB cannot be rendered (none of the corpus, see Measurements). Transparency groups and layers are not counted.
- Each paint of a mesh shading samples it afresh (hayro keeps no sampled mesh), so a page painting one large mesh many times costs up to `MAX_SAMPLE_WORK` a paint, within the pixmap budget.
- Sampled (type 0) functions with many inputs evaluate 2^inputs table corners; with a large enough table a DeviceN image in such a space is slow. Not reached by the corpus.
- Open outliers past 10× MuPDF, unchanged by this work: pdf.js `large_jpeg_downscale.pdf` (render, image work) and veraPDF `isartor-6-1-12-t01-fail-a.pdf` (`text-all`, text piece); see Root causes.
